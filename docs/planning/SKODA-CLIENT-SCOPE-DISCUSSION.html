1 · The approach, in plain termswhat we're building and why it's a good fit

What changes

  • Both sites move to Edge Delivery Services — Storyboard (editorial) and Media Room (pressroom), one platform.
  • Authored in Experience Workspace — a modern WYSIWYG authoring workspace with built-in AI support; no heavy CMS to run.
  • Pages are static and fast — served from Adobe's CDN, built for strong Core Web Vitals.
  • Media comes from AEM Assets — one approved source of images; the site generates the right sizes automatically.
  • Listings & search from a content index — homepage rails, filters and listings populate themselves from your tags.

Why it helps

  • No plugin sprawl — the current site carries a large custom WordPress + jQuery stack; the rebuild is clean and maintainable.
  • Fast by default — static delivery, images optimised at request time, no server rendering to wait on.
  • Author once, reuse everywhere — the same building blocks serve every page type on both sites.
  • A repeatable method — the pipeline and patterns here become the blueprint for future VW Edge Delivery sites.
This is a fresh build of both sites. Before proposing dates, we ran a rapid proof with real Škoda content to measure the two things that usually decide a migration's schedule: how fast content imports, and how fast pages come together. Both are fast, which is why the plan below is realistic.

2 · Why the timeline is realisticwhat the rapid proof measured, before committing to dates

We didn't estimate on paper. We took real Škoda stories and media through the pipeline and assembled sample pages, to measure speed and confirm the harder functions are achievable on the new platform.

  • Import is fast — real stories and images pulled through an automated pipeline and published, so bulk migration is a measured quantity, not a guess.
  • The build is quick — page types assemble from a small set of reusable blocks, so most new pages are configuration, not custom development.
  • The tricky parts are feasible — the three functions flagged as not off-the-shelf (excluding items from listings, placing a story in more than one category, and mixing manual picks with tag-based recommendations) all work cleanly here.
  • Performance holds up — static delivery plus request-time image optimisation give the Core Web Vitals headroom the current site lacks.
The takeaway: there's no research risk left in the approach. The demo and go-live below are an assembly-and-migration exercise on proven foundations, which is what makes 15 October and 02 January achievable.

5 · What Edge Delivery doesn't do out of the boxthe few non-native pieces, and how we cover each

Edge Delivery is a static platform, which is what makes it fast. A handful of things sit outside that model and need a small companion service or one of your existing systems. We'd rather name them up front than have them surface late.

Needs a small companion service

  • Zipping an arbitrary cart selection — a static site can't package a journalist's ad-hoc pick into one ZIP on the fly. A small service (App Builder + AEM Assets) does the size-reduction and packaging. Demo fallback: the selected files download separately. A whole press-kit ZIP is different, it's a pre-built file, so that one is easy.
  • Search & filtering at full library scale — the content index handles listings and index-based search well for a representative set; true search and faceting across tens of thousands of assets, with ranking, wants a hosted search service. The demo proves the experience on a sample.
  • Transcoding non-image originals — the platform resizes and reformats images natively (it already serves smaller web renditions on the fly), but it doesn't process PDFs or video files; those are referenced from the DAM as-is.
  • Gated end-user access (the QR journey) — there's no built-in per-visitor gating; this needs a defined access model and likely a small service. One to design together.

Stays with your existing systems

  • Cookie consent — your OneTrust setup stays; we integrate with it rather than rebuild it.
  • Newsletter / subscription — no built-in mail platform; we wire your provider to the sign-up form.
  • Analytics — the event layer connects to your analytics platform.
  • Third-party embeds — the AI audio reading and video players are kept as the embeds they already are, not rebuilt.
None of these is a surprise or a blocker. They're the known edges of a static platform, each with a clear plan. The demo shows the cart working end to end; production-grade packaging and full-scale search are part of the go-live build.
